1Prerequisite 1 · Transport

The race

  • Oocyte swept to ampulla
    At ovulation the secondary oocyte is expelled into the uterine tube. Fluid currents from cilia and tubal peristalsis sweep it to the ampulla.
  • Hundreds of millions in
    Lecture: 200–600 million sperm deposited in the posterior fornix of the vagina (Langman 15e: 200–300 million).
    Many die in the acidic vagina; cervical mucus slows the rest; uterine & tubal muscular contractions carry them up.
  • Only a few hundred arrive
    Lecture: ≈200 reach the ampulla. Langman 15e: 300–500 reach the site of fertilization.
  • Fertile for 2–5 days
    Stored mainly in the tail of the epididymis → peristalsis of the vas deferens → urethra.
    Fertilizing power lasts 2–5 days (lecture).
Lecture: Fertilization & First Week, slides 4–6 · Langman 15e, Ch. 3
1Prerequisites 2 & 3

Getting ready

Capacitation

Proteins stripped off the sperm head · 5–7 h

Removal of proteins from the plasma membrane over the sperm head → sperm becomes more active and able to fertilize.
Occurs in the female genital tract (lecture: starts at the cervix; Langman: mainly in the uterine tube, ≈7 h).
Only capacitated sperm can fertilize.

Acrosome reaction

Acrosome releases enzymes at the egg coats

Hyaluronidase → corona radiata
Trypsin-like enzymes → digest zona pellucida
Acrosin (zona lysin) → softens zona pellucida
Only capacitated sperm undergo it (Langman: triggered by zona proteins).
Lecture: Fertilization & First Week, slides 7–8 · Langman 15e, Ch. 3
1Phases of fertilization

Six phases

  1. Corona radiataHyaluronidase · tubal enzymes · tail beats
  2. Zona pellucidaZona reaction blocks other sperm
  3. Membranes fuseHead & tail enter; sperm membrane left behind
  4. Meiosis II completesFemale pronucleus (22,X) + 2nd polar body
  5. Male pronucleus22,X or 22,Y · tail degenerates
  6. Pronuclei uniteDNA replicated → zygote, 46 chromosomes
Lecture: Fertilization & First Week, slides 9–13 · Langman 15e, Ch. 3 · “22,X” = 22 autosomes + X (23 chromosomes)
1Consequences

What fertilization does

  • Completes meiosis II
    The secondary oocyte is stimulated to finish its 2nd meiotic division.
  • Blocks polyspermy
    Sperm–oocyte fusion → Ca²⁺ rise → cortical granules release enzymes → zona glycoproteins & sperm receptors modified → zona hardens → other sperm can’t enter.
  • Restores 46 chromosomes
    Two haploid pronuclei → diploid zygote (46).
  • Determines sex
    X-bearing sperm → female (46,XX)
    Y-bearing sperm → male (46,XY)
  • Creates variation
    Thorough shuffling of maternal & paternal chromosomes.
  • Starts cleavage
    Initiates mitotic division of the zygote.

1Days 1–3

Cleavage

  • Starts within 30 h
    Cleavage = the onset of mitosis in the zygote, beginning within ~30 hours of fertilization.
  • In the uterine tube
    Occurs as the zygote moves along the tube during the first ~3 days.
  • Smaller blastomeres
    2 → 4 → 8 → 16 cells; each division halves the cytoplasm — the embryo does not grow.
  • Morula enters uterus
    16-cell stage reaches the uterine cavity ≈ day 3–4 (lecture: day 3; Langman: ~day 4).
    Zona pellucida still surrounds it.

Implantation

Blastocyst embeds in the functional endometrium

Where mesoderm can’t go

5Amniotic cavity

Amniotic fluid

  • Four functions
    • Protective cushion
    • Prevents adherence of embryo to amnion
    • Allows fetal movement
    • Helps dilate the cervix in labour
  • Swallowed ≈ 400 mL/day
    From month 5 the fetus swallows its own fluid — ~400 mL/day at term (about half). Fetal urine (mostly water) is added.
  • Replaced every 3 h
  • Fills the chorionic cavity
    By the end of month 3 the expanding amniotic cavity obliterates the chorionic cavity.

6Structure

Two parts

  • Fetal: chorion frondosum
    Chorionic villi opposite the decidua basalis.
    Anchoring villi reach through to the decidua; free (nourishing) villi float in the intervillous space.
  • Maternal: decidua basalis
    Forms the maternal part; placental septa project from it as incomplete walls.
  • 15–20 cotyledons
    Separated by deep grooves made by the placental septa. Intervillous spaces communicate with each other.
  • Grows by branching
    Size ↑ by new villi; thickness ↑ by arborization of villi. Microvilli raise the surface area to 4–14 m².
Lecture: Placenta, slides 7–12 · Langman 15e, Ch. 8 (Fig. 8.13) · hover the structures
6Circulation

Two bloods, never mixing

  • 80–100 spiral arteries
    Eroded by villi; arterial blood (higher pressure) spurts into the intervillous space carrying O₂, nutrients & antibodies, then drains to endometrial veins (lower pressure) carrying CO₂ & waste.
  • ≈150 mL in the spaces
    Intervillous spaces of the mature placenta hold about 150 mL of maternal blood.
  • Umbilical vein → fetus
    Carries O₂ and nutrients to the fetus.
  • Umbilical arteries → placenta
    Carry fetal CO₂ and waste back to the placenta. Exchange happens across the villus wall — the placental barrier.
Lecture: Placenta, slides 13–15 · Langman 15e, Ch. 8

6Clinical

Preeclampsia

  • Maternal hypertension
    Can progress to eclampsia — seizures.
  • IUGR · fetal or maternal death
    Serious outcomes for both mother and fetus.
  • Cured by delivery
    Completely reversible by delivery of the baby.
  • Risk factors
    • Preeclampsia in a previous pregnancy
    • First pregnancy
    • Obesity
    • Family history
    • Multiple gestation
    • Hypertension · diabetes

6Umbilical cord

The lifeline

  • 30–80 cm · 2 cm wide
    Average length 50 cm. Spirally twisted (false knots) and covered by amnion.
  • 2 arteries + 1 vein
    • Wharton’s jelly
    • 2 umbilical arteries
    • 1 umbilical vein
    • Allantois (urachus)
    • Remains of the vitelline duct
  • Long or short cord
    Long cord → true knots, loop around the neck.
    Short cord → abruptio placentae.
  • Single artery: 1 in 200
    ~20% of these babies have cardiac, renal or other vascular defects.

7Twinning

Two from one pregnancy

  • Dizygotic: 2 ova + 2 sperm
    Like ordinary siblings — sex may differ. Each has its own chorion & amnion → DCDA; placentas separate or fused.

7Monochorionic twins

Why chorion matters

  • One placenta, linked vessels
    Monochorionic twins share one placenta → vascular anastomoses between the two circulations.
  • Feto-fetal transfusion
    Donor → oligohydramnios, IUGR
    Recipient → polyhydramnios, polycythemia, heart failure, hydrops
    Chorionicity is judged most reliably by early ultrasound.
  • TAPS
    Twin anemia–polycythemia sequence: chronic, unequal red-cell transfer.
  • MCMA → cord entanglement
    Both fetuses share one amniotic cavity.

8Genetic factors

Written in the genes

  • Chromosomal
    Common syndromes: Down, Edwards (trisomy 18 — 2nd most common trisomy, ~80% female, most die before birth).
    Microdeletions: DiGeorge (22q11), cri-du-chat (5p), Prader-Willi, Angelman.
  • Single-gene
    Isolated: hydrocephalus, microcephaly, aniridia, cataract, polydactyly.
    Syndromes: Apert (FGFR2), ectrodactyly–ectodermal dysplasia, Roberts, Van der Woude (IRF6).
  • Gene over-expression
    Dicephalus — over-expression of GOOSECOID.
  • Multifactorial
    The majority of genetic malformations; many loci (polygenic), e.g. type 1 diabetes.
  • Genetic heterogeneity
    One malformation, many causes (chromosomal, syndromic or isolated): holoprosencephaly, cleft lip/palate, NTDs.
